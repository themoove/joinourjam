The run

Six moves. You own the music.

The year, planned with you: strategy, optimisation, and business development that stay yours. The route is part of that plan, not the whole of it. Nothing here is a promise of a result.

  1. 01

    The songs

    The tracks, the set, the pace. Built around you, not a pack.

  2. 02

    Tour-ready

    A plain read of the work, the listeners, and the plan you will run. Strategy and optimisation, worked over to you. If it is early, you hear it.

  3. 03

    Your rights

    Splits, registrations, the paperwork. The songs stay yours.

  4. 04

    The campaign

    Your name, the artwork, how the season is told. Tied to the dates.

  5. 05

    The fans

    Where they already are, before a favourite city gets a date.

  6. 06

    The tour deal

    Dates, fees, terms, from the fan regions out. When CITY JAM does this, CITY JAM receives 25% of the tour revenue. Outside the monthly fee. Not assumed.

On the record

What you get.

Four pieces with names you already use. None of them is a surprise inside the monthly fee, and none of them is a promise.

01

The tour plan

Business development with you: strategy, optimisation, and the plan you keep. The route sits inside that — fan region first, tour destination second — it is not a list of dates on its own. Wanting a city is not the same as being called there.

02

Socials and the release

The posts, the pitch, and the terms around the release, when that work is on. It sits with Presence and Bespoke. Both are still proposed.

03

Sync and licensing

Sync and other licence requests for music you own. Separate from the monthly fee, and not assumed. A request is not a closed licence.

04

Your catalogue

Songs you have already made, put to work for the next run. If a catalogue is bought or sold, that is its own piece of work. It is not a promise either way.

When CITY JAM negotiates a tour, CITY JAM receives 25% of the tour revenue. That fee is not part of the monthly membership, and it is not assumed. If we cannot do the work, we would rather say so.
